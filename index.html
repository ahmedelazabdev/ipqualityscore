Understanding IP Quality Scores: How Fraud Detection Algorithms Work

In modern cybersecurity, e-commerce, and fraud prevention, an IP Quality Score (IPQS) evaluates how trustworthy an Internet Protocol address is. Every time a user places an online purchase, registers an account, or submits a form, anti-fraud algorithms inspect the connecting IP to detect whether traffic originates from a legitimate human residential connection or an anonymizing proxy network.

💡 The Core Threat Metric: Fraud scores range from 0 to 100. Legitimate residential home internet connections typically score between 0 and 15. Scores above 75 indicate high-risk traffic, commercial VPN tunnels, Tor exit nodes, or infected machines involved in distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) and credential stuffing attacks.

Why Do Legitimate Users Sometimes Get High Fraud Scores?

A high IP fraud score does not always mean you are a hacker. Several common configurations can trigger false positives:

  1. Commercial VPNs: If you use services like NordVPN, ExpressVPN, or Cloudflare WARP, your traffic exits through a shared data center hosting provider. Anti-fraud systems treat all data center traffic with elevated caution.
  2. Apple iCloud Private Relay: macOS and iOS users with Private Relay enabled route Safari traffic through dual-hop proxies, which can occasionally flag an IP as anonymized.
  3. Infected Smart Devices (IoT): On shared household Wi-Fi networks, a compromised smart TV or malware-infected PC might be sending spam quietly, causing your public IP to land on real-time DNS blocklists.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What score is considered safe for online payments and banking? +
Scores between 0 and 25 are considered completely clean and safe. Payment gateways (such as Stripe, PayPal, and Adyen) typically approve these transactions instantly without requiring secondary verification (3D Secure SMS OTPs).
Can I check both IPv4 and IPv6 addresses? +
Yes. The checker accepts standard 32-bit IPv4 addresses (e.g., 8.8.8.8) and 128-bit hexadecimal IPv6 addresses (e.g., 2001:4860:4860::8888), extracting routing ASN data for both protocols.
How often are IP blacklist records updated? +
Threat feeds and DNSBL tables update dynamically every 15 to 60 minutes. If your IP was recently cleaned or you rebooted your router to get a new IP lease, the updated status reflects almost immediately.
